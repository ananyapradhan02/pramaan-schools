what a pilot is

  1. week 0

    set-up

    one hour with you and the teacher leads. we pick the sections and the chapters, send a parent note in english and kannada, and collect consent before any questionnaire.

  2. weeks 1 to 8

    sessions

    two sessions a week per section, co-taught with your teacher so the method stays in the school after we leave. a two-minute pulse each week, and the full questionnaires in weeks one and eight.

  3. week 8

    report and review

    you get the outcome report and forty-five minutes with us to read it together and decide whether to continue, widen or stop.

what your school gets

an outcome report

the outcome report, built on three questionnaires

we did not invent a scale. each measure comes from published research, and the report names its source.

curiosity cei-ii

Curiosity and Exploration Inventory II

ten items from psychologist Todd Kashdan, who studies curiosity and well-being at George Mason University, and his colleagues. it reads two things: stretching (seeking out new knowledge and experience) and embracing (staying with the new and uncertain instead of avoiding it). five-point scale.

Kashdan, T. B., Gallagher, M. W., Silvia, P. J., et al. (2009). The Curiosity and Exploration Inventory-II. Journal of Research in Personality, 43(6), 987-998.

motivation imi

Intrinsic Motivation Inventory, short form

from the self-determination theory research of Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, psychologists at the University of Rochester who showed that people learn best when they feel choice, competence and connection. we use four subscales: interest and enjoyment, perceived competence, perceived choice, and pressure (reverse scored). seven-point scale.

Ryan, R. M. (1982). Control and information in the intrapersonal sphere. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 43(3), 450-461. Scale and scoring: selfdeterminationtheory.org.

self-efficacy pals

academic efficacy items, Patterns of Adaptive Learning Scales

five items written for middle-school students by Carol Midgley, an educational psychologist at the University of Michigan, and her team. they rest on the work of Albert Bandura, the Stanford psychologist who defined self-efficacy: a student's belief that they can do the work in front of them. five-point scale.

Midgley, C., Maehr, M. L., Hruda, L. Z., et al. (2000). Manual for the Patterns of Adaptive Learning Scales. University of Michigan. Bandura, A. (1997). Self-efficacy: the exercise of control. W. H. Freeman.

what the report can say: how each section's average moved over eight weeks, and how confident we are in that movement given how many students answered.

what it cannot say: that pramaan caused the change. a pilot has no comparison group, and these are self-report questionnaires. we read items aloud in english or kannada where it helps, and note every adaptation in the report.

what we never do

  • no child is named, scored or ranked. the report and the dashboard show class averages only.
  • no class figure is shown when fewer than ten students answered that week.
  • no leaderboards, and no screen tells a child how they did.
  • no child data leaves the school. questionnaires are on paper or the school's own device, and we take away class totals.
  • parental consent for every child, because the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 treats everyone under eighteen as a child.

    how to read this

    higher means more: more curious, more interested, more sure they can do the work. each line is a class average on the questionnaire's own scale, so the curiosity and self-efficacy charts run from one to five and motivation runs from one to seven.

    a change is a movement in a class, not a grade for a child. with no comparison group, read it as a signal to talk about with your teachers, not as proof.
